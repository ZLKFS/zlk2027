Courland sklandrausis in Sweden

Recipe: Sanita Heinberga, Järna, and Agnese Blaubārde, Stockholm · ≈ 2 h 30 min

Only one recipe reached us twice – and that was the Courland sklandrausis! The recipes were sent in by two very special Courlanders in Sweden. Sanita Heinberga, originally from Ventspils, now lives in Järna near Södertälje and is a professional pastry chef. Designer Agnese Blaubārde comes from Pāvilosta but calls Stockholm and the Latvian School in Stockholm home. Here is how they describe their relationship with sklandrausis. Sanita: “As a true Courlander, baking sklandrausis is a matter of honour for me and a mark of respect for Latvia's history. With its bright colour, sklandrausis reminds me of the sun – it embodies warmth, light and love for my roots.” She reminds us that sklandrausis is also called dižrausis and žograusis. Agnese: “I chose this recipe as a Courlander out in the world. Alsunga sklandrausis were always baked by my grandmother Ruta. For me they belong with her and with home. That taste I want to keep and pass on.”

Ingredients

  • Pastry base
  • Potato filling
  • Carrot filling
  • For glazing

Method

  1. 1Add the butter and salt to warm water, then the flour, and mix. Agnese advises adding the water little by little so there is not too much!
  2. 2Roll the dough out to about 2 mm.
  3. 3Cut out round bases 10–15 cm across.
  4. 4Fold the edges up about 2 cm to form a “fence”.
  5. 5Boil the potatoes and mash or grate them smooth. Work in the melted butter, soured cream, eggs and salt.
  6. 6Boil the carrots and mash or grate them smooth. Work in the soured cream, eggs, sugar and salt.
  7. 7Spread the potato mixture in the bases first, then the carrot mixture on top. Agnese sprinkles caraway over before baking.
  8. 8Bake in a hot oven, 220–250 °C, for about 15–30 minutes (or at a lower 200 °C for about 40 minutes!), until the pastry base is dry.
  9. 9Brush the finished sklandrausis with a mixture of soured cream, egg and sugar. In Agnese's recipe you may instead put a tablespoon of soured cream on each one and sprinkle with sugar (no egg!).
  10. 10In Sanita's recipe the sklandrausis are sprinkled with cinnamon or caraway, as you prefer.

We suggest trying both recipes and making your own versions!
