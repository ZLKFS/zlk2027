Curd cheese crescents, Smiltene style

Recipe: Elīna Apsīte, Knivsta · ≈ 50 min

This recipe comes from Elīna Apsīte, who lives in Knivsta in Uppsala County but comes from Smiltene. The cafés of Smiltene served these curd cheese crescents – biezpiena radziņi – which you will hardly find anywhere else in Latvia! Elīna experimented for a long time before she found the true taste of her childhood. She loves anything with biezpiens, the Latvian curd cheese – in her view Latvia's real superfood. Luckily her local ICA now stocks curd cheese from Poland. Elīna treats Swedish friends and colleagues to the crescents and brings them to the Latvian School in Stockholm; her daughter Emma has sold them at the school's Michaelmas fair. Warm crescents always vanish in a flash!

Ingredients for about 20 crescents

Method

  1. 1Put the curd cheese and soft butter in a bowl and mash and mix thoroughly with a fork until smooth.
  2. 2Add the flour and baking powder and knead by hand into a soft, even dough.
  3. 3Cover with a tea towel and let the dough rest for 10–15 minutes.
  4. 4Roll the dough out to about 3–5 mm and sprinkle evenly with sugar.
  5. 5Cut the dough into triangles. The easy way is to cut out a round and slice it like a pizza. Make the triangles whatever size you like.
  6. 6Shape the crescents: roll each triangle from the wide edge towards the point into a little horn.
  7. 7For sweeter, crunchier biscuits, roll the crescents in sugar before baking.
  8. 8Place on a baking tray lined with baking paper and bake in an oven preheated to 180 °C for about 10–15 minutes, until lightly golden.
  9. 9Let them cool a little and enjoy with tea, coffee or milk.

Elīna's tips: If the curd cheese is very grainy, press it through a sieve or blend it before making the dough, for softer biscuits. You can roll small pieces of fruit jelly into the triangles – the Finnish marmalade sold at ICA is close to the Latvian kind.
