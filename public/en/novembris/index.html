Speķa pīrāgi – Latvian bacon buns

Recipe: Laila Strunke, Stockholm · ≈ 2 h 30 min

Stockholmer Laila Strunke's mother was an outstanding cook. Laila may well have inherited the cooking gene from her – but not the recipes. Her mother knew her important recipes by heart, and over the years and the moves the many cookbooks were lost too. Here is how Laila bakes pīrāgi, adapting a recipe from santa.lv to Swedish conditions.

Ingredients for about 40 buns

Method

  1. 1Start the yeast: crumble it, add a teaspoon of sugar, a little warm water or milk and about a tablespoon of flour. Mix to a paste and set in a warm place.
  2. 2Pour the milk into a pan, add the butter, sugar and salt and heat, stirring all the time so the milk does not get too hot.
  3. 3If the milk does boil, it must be cooled, because the yeast must not go into hot milk.
  4. 4When the butter has melted, take the pan off the heat, pour into a large bowl and sift in flour. Stir with a wooden spoon until the dough has the consistency of thick cream.
  5. 5By now the yeast has risen – stir it in too and keep sifting in flour and stirring.
  6. 6When the dough is thick enough, continue kneading by hand – until it is smooth, glossy and comes away from your hands and the bowl. Knead it hard – with the palm, with the fist, and by slapping it around.
  7. 7When the dough is kneaded, dust it lightly with flour, cover with a cloth and leave in a warm place to rise. It is ready when it has doubled in size!
  8. 8While the dough rises, make the filling: chop the meat and onion finely. Fry the onion first in a little fat until golden and translucent, then add the meat and fry only briefly – just until the pieces no longer stick together. Add a little pepper and salt.
  9. 9Shape the buns. Take a piece of dough, roll or press it thin, put filling in the middle, fold the other half over and press the bun out with a glass. Pinch the edges firmly with your fingers and tuck the seam under the bun – so it sits against the tray. Mind the dough is not too thick!
  10. 10Beat the egg and brush the buns so they come out browner and shinier.
  11. 11Put them in the oven at once, so they do not keep rising and open up! Bake in the upper part of the oven at a high heat – 250 °C for 7–8 minutes.

Laila's tip: This dough also works for tray bakes and buns.
