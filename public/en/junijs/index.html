Quick Jāņi cheese from Auči – Latvian midsummer cheese

Recipe: Šarlote Cērps, Stockholm · ≈ 1 h

Šarlote Cērps is a Stockholmer with family roots in Kuldīga, Riga and Rūjiena. She learned to make Jāņi cheese – the Latvian midsummer cheese with caraway – while visiting a Latvian family in the USA who hosted the midsummer celebration for the local Latvians. The recipe with 8 litres of milk never made it into her repertoire, though. This is a brand-new recipe that Šarlote adapted from the 2026 “SestDiena” magazine, when she celebrated midsummer in Latvia, at Auči in Salgale parish, and every cheese turned out to be sold out! Šarlote's cheese was also praised by the singers of the Latvian choir in Stockholm, whom she treated to it in October. Because this treat can be enjoyed in any season!

Ingredients for about 6 servings

Method

  1. 1Pour a little water into the bottom of a pot so nothing catches, then add the milk and spices.
  2. 2Heat to just below boiling, 80–85 °C.
  3. 3While the milk heats, beat the egg, 1 tsp salt and the soured cream in a bowl. (You can use whipping cream and add 1 tsp of vinegar.)
  4. 4When the milk is hot, take the pot off the heat and pour in the egg and cream mixture.
  5. 5Return the pot to the heat and keep heating until the milk curdles and the whey separates. This happens quickly once it reaches 85 °C.
  6. 6Take the pot off the heat and wait about 5 minutes.
  7. 7Line a sieve with a double layer of cheesecloth and drain off the whey; let it drip for a few minutes.
  8. 8Stir the butter into the curds and add more salt to taste.
  9. 9Keep draining for at least 30 minutes.
  10. 10Squeeze out as much liquid as you can, put the cheese, cloth and all, in a suitable dish, weigh it down and leave for a few hours in a cool place.

Šarlote recommends serving the cheese with Finn Crisp crispbread and radishes. The cheese weighs about 300 g, enough for roughly 16 slices of Finn Crisp.

She also suggests using the whey – as fertiliser for plants, as the liquid in bread baking, for Scandinavian whey butter, messmör, or as the base of a cold vegetable soup or smoothie. But that is another recipe!
